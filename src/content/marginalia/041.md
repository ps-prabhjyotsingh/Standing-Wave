E07 says clever code belongs behind a dull door: sealed in a library with a boring
interface that nobody needs to read. I mostly agree, but the door has a cost the essay does
not count. A clever line is usually the answer to a constraint, and the constraint is
recorded nowhere except in the shape of the trick. The essay's own example, the inverse
square root with the magic constant, was a fine answer where division and square roots were
slow; later processors gained one instruction that makes the same estimate. Nothing behind
a dull door gets that news. Not needing to be read is the door's whole virtue, and it is
also how a trick outlives its reason.
