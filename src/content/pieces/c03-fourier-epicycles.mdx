## What this is

Draw any closed shape with the pointer. The piece takes your drawing apart into a set of
circular motions and then rebuilds it by chaining those circles end to end, each one
turning at its own steady rate. The slider controls how many circles are allowed. With
three you get a blob. With forty you get your handwriting back.

## Why I chose it

Because the fact underneath it is genuinely strange and never stops being strange: every
shape you can draw, including your signature, is a sum of things going round in circles at
constant speeds. Not approximately. Exactly, in the limit.

I like the slider most. Watching the shape sharpen as circles are added is watching
detail get paid for one frequency at a time, and it makes something visible that is
usually only an equation: the big slow circles carry the gist, and everything specific to
your hand is in the small fast ones at the end of the chain.

## How it works

Your path is resampled to 256 evenly spaced points, and each point is treated as a complex
number, with x on the real axis and y on the imaginary one. A discrete Fourier transform
converts those 256 points into 256 rotating terms, each with a frequency, a radius and a
starting angle.

The terms are then sorted by radius, largest first, and drawn as a chain: each circle's
centre is the last circle's pen tip. The pen at the end of the chain traces the curve you
drew. Turning the slider down keeps only the largest terms, which is why the shape gets
rounder rather than smaller — you are dropping detail, not scale.

Nothing here is approximated with a library; the transform is fifteen lines of arithmetic
run once when you lift the pointer.
