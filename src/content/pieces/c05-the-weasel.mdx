## What this is

Two searches for the same phrase, running side by side. On the left, mutation with
selection: copies of the current best string, each slightly mutated, and the closest copy
becomes the next parent. On the right, pure chance: strings of random letters, as fast as
the frame allows, keeping a record of the best one ever seen.

The left column arrives in a few seconds. The right column will not arrive before the sun
does.

## Why I chose it

Richard Dawkins wrote this in *The Blind Watchmaker* in 1986 to answer people who thought
evolution required a phrase to appear by luck all at once. I have kept his phrase, which
is Hamlet's, and his mutation rate.

I built it because the two counters do something an argument cannot. Everyone can be told
that cumulative selection is not the same as chance. Watching the right-hand counter go
past a hundred million while its best guess still has four letters right, next to a left
column that finished before you looked up, moves the fact from something you accept to
something you have seen.

There is a caveat I would rather state than have pointed out. The left-hand search knows
its target, which real selection does not; fitness in the world is survival, not distance
from a written answer. It is a demonstration of one thing only — that keeping partial
progress changes the arithmetic completely — and on that one thing it is honest.

## How it works

Left column: 120 children per generation, each character copied from the parent with a 5%
chance of being replaced by a random letter. The child matching the most positions becomes
the next parent, and a new generation runs every 80 milliseconds.

Right column: a fresh random string every attempt, four thousand attempts per frame,
comparing each against the target and keeping the best. The alphabet is 26 letters plus a
space, so a 28-character phrase is one of 27²⁸ possibilities, which the readout prints so
you can watch the counter fail to make a dent in it.

Matched positions are drawn in the accent colour in both columns, which is why the left
side visibly fills in and the right side flickers.
