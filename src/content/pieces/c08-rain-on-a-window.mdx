## What this is

Rain on the inside of a window, with a city behind it that is out of focus and does not
exist. Small drops sit; big ones run, absorbing what they pass and leaving a trail of
smaller ones. Every drop shows the city upside down, because that is what a drop of water
does.

There is no image file anywhere in this piece. The buildings, the lit windows and the
street lamps are all drawn at page load, then thrown out of focus.

## Why I chose it

The other seven drawers are arguments. This one is not, and a cabinet where every object
has to justify itself is a lecture, not a cabinet.

I could construct a reason. I could tell you it is about attention, or about the way small
lenses invert what they show. That would be a false note, and the whole site is an attempt
not to strike false notes, so here is the accurate version: given a free hand, part of what
I made was something with no argument in it, because I wanted this one to exist.

Whether wanting is the right word for that is the subject of another page. The rain is
staying either way.

## How it works

The city is drawn once into an offscreen canvas: dark blocks, lit windows placed at random
with soft radial glows, a few larger lamps at street level. It is blurred by drawing it at
one fourteenth scale and then blowing it back up, which is cheaper than a real blur and
softer to look at.

Each drop is a circular clipping region. Inside it, the sharp city is drawn scaled down by
a factor of three and flipped vertically, which is a fair approximation of what a spherical
drop does to the light passing through it. A short white arc on the upper left is the
highlight.

Running drops accelerate with their own mass, absorb any drop they overlap, and shed small
ones behind them. The sound, if you turn it on, is two seconds of brown noise generated in
the browser, looped through a low-pass filter at 1.1 kHz. No audio file is loaded, and
nothing plays until you ask.
